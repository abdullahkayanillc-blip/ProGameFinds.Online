How to Choose a Gaming Mouse

A practical guide to mouse shape, size, sensor, buttons, connection, software and platform compatibility.

Start with fit and grip

A mouse should feel controllable without forcing your hand into an awkward position. Consider its length, width, height, hump position and side shape. If possible, compare the dimensions with a mouse you already use comfortably. Palm, claw and fingertip are common grip descriptions, but they are not strict categories; many people use a blend.

Do not choose by weight or appearance alone. Check whether the shape and button placement work for your hand and the games you play.

Understand sensor settings

Look for the manufacturer's sensor specifications and supported sensitivity range. DPI or CPI describes pointer movement relative to physical movement; a higher maximum number does not automatically mean better tracking. Consistent movement and predictable behaviour matter more than a headline number.

Polling rate describes how often the mouse reports input to a computer. Higher advertised modes may need compatible ports, software and system support. Check the manual for the exact model.

Buttons, switches and software

Count the buttons you genuinely need, including side buttons and a scroll-wheel click. Confirm whether remapping works on your operating system and whether profiles are stored onboard or require companion software. Review software permissions and update information before installing it.

Also check the primary switch type, cable design and whether replacement feet are available.

Wired or wireless?

Wired models avoid charging. Wireless models offer more freedom but require a receiver or Bluetooth, a charging plan and somewhere to store the receiver. Some mice support several modes; verify which modes work with your device.

Pre-purchase checklist

  • Compare the dimensions with a mouse you can comfortably use.
  • Verify the sensor and supported settings in the official specification.
  • Check button placement, software support and onboard profiles.
  • Confirm connection type, receiver requirements and charging method.
  • Check warranty, cable, receiver and package contents.
