How to Choose a Gaming Mouse

A gaming mouse should fit your hand and the games you play. A high DPI number or a long feature list does not automatically make a mouse the right choice. Start with fit, then compare the features that affect your actual use.

Editorial guide · Published November 14, 2026 · Not a hands-on product test or a guarantee of product performance.

Start with grip and shape

Palm, claw and fingertip grips place the hand differently on the mouse. Shapes also vary in width, height, hump position and side contour. If possible, compare dimensions with a mouse you already find comfortable. Do not assume that a mouse marketed for a particular grip will fit every hand in that category.

Treat DPI as a range, not a score

DPI describes cursor sensitivity. A higher maximum number is not proof of better tracking or better aim. Look for clear sensor information, adjustable settings and consistent tracking reports from independent sources. Choose a sensitivity that feels controllable rather than chasing the largest specification.

Consider weight and buttons

Weight is a preference and depends on the games and movements you make. Extra buttons can help with shortcuts, but only if they are easy to reach without changing your grip. Check whether software is required for configuration and whether it supports your operating system.

Wired or wireless

Wired models avoid battery charging and need no wireless receiver. Wireless models reduce cable drag but add charging, receiver placement and battery considerations. Confirm the exact connection modes and included receiver before buying.

Final checklist

Check the exact model number, dimensions, return policy, warranty region, included accessories and seller. Compare the manufacturer's product page with the retailer listing. Never rely on a product title alone if multiple revisions share a similar name.

Before you buy

Verify the exact model and current details with the manufacturer and retailer. Prices, availability, sellers, product revisions and warranty terms can change. Do not rely on an old screenshot or a similarly named model.
