How to Choose a Gaming Headset

A headset is worn for long sessions, so comfort and microphone quality can matter as much as sound. Decide which devices you need it to work with before comparing surround-sound labels or extra features.

Editorial guide · Published November 14, 2026 · Not a hands-on product test or a guarantee of product performance.

Fit and comfort

Check headset weight, headband adjustment, ear-cup depth and material. Glasses, ear shape and room temperature can affect comfort. Reviews about long-session comfort are more useful when they describe the reviewer’s fit and use rather than making a universal claim.

Microphone needs

If teammates need to hear you clearly, look for real microphone samples recorded in ordinary rooms. A detachable or flip-to-mute microphone may be convenient. Noise reduction claims vary, so listen to samples instead of relying only on marketing terms.

Wired and wireless options

Wired headsets avoid charging and may connect through USB or a 3.5 mm jack. Wireless headsets may use a USB receiver or Bluetooth; these are not interchangeable. Check battery-life test conditions and whether the microphone works over every advertised connection.

Platform compatibility

Confirm the exact console, PC, phone or handheld device. Some features may require software or may work only on a particular platform. A headset fitting a port does not guarantee that every control or feature is supported.

Final checks

Compare return policy, warranty, replaceable ear cushions, cable type and included adapters. Verify the exact model and regional version on the manufacturer's website before ordering.

Before you buy

Verify the exact model and current details with the manufacturer and retailer. Prices, availability, sellers, product revisions and warranty terms can change. Do not rely on an old screenshot or a similarly named model.
